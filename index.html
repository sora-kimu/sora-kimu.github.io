▸ Responsibility is produced, not assigned. +

"Produced" means responsibility emerges through organizational processes — documentation regimes, investigative systems, attribution rituals — not pre-existing and merely labeled.

This framing shifts the design question from "who is responsible?" to "how does this system produce responsibility?" — and opens new design possibilities, including for AI systems entering regulated work.

Theoretical lineage: Garfinkel (accountability), Star & Ruhleder (infrastructure), Suchman (situated action), Orlikowski (sociomateriality).

▸ RESEARCH FOCUS +

Regulated work environments shape how people inside them think, decide, and bear responsibility — and AI is now reshaping all three. Few study how either works.

Regulated environments — pharmaceutical manufacturing, clinical healthcare, aviation, financial compliance — produce specific cognitive, decision-making, and accountability conditions for the people inside them. AI is now entering these environments — sometimes amplifying existing problems, sometimes opening new design possibilities.

My research builds frameworks and tools that do two things at once: improve the working environment for practitioners (accessibility, navigability, coordination, reasoning support) and make accountability structures legible so that responsibility is distributed honestly rather than collapsed onto individuals retroactively.

The two are inseparable: the same design choices that determine what workers can see, do, and decide also determine how responsibility gets produced and assigned. The Regulatory HCI framework grounds this argument in four interaction layers (accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning), each tested through a deployed prototype. The accountability lifecycle (AAC → CF → RC → PAC) traces how responsibility is structurally produced before, during, and after action — including under AI mediation.

Orientation: This work is practitioner-oriented. My audience is the people who actually build and use these tools inside organizations — not ethicists or philosophers. I don't argue regulation should be stronger or weaker. I ask: regardless of how regulation is structured, who designs the environment for the people who must operate within it? And when AI enters that environment, what does it look like to design it so that human judgment and accountability are preserved rather than collapsed?

📖 Glossary
Terms used across this research
GMP Practice
▸GMPGood Manufacturing Practice
FDA-enforced standards for pharmaceutical production. Covers documentation, personnel training, facility design, equipment qualification, and quality systems. Non-compliance results in Warning Letters or facility shutdown.
In this researchGMP serves as the empirical site for studying how accountability structures are produced in regulated work.
▸SOPStandard Operating Procedure
Formal documents specifying step-by-step procedures for regulated activities. Following an SOP is both operational guidance and legal protection — practitioners who deviate bear responsibility for outcomes.
In this researchSOPs are studied as part of the structural conditions that shape interpretation and produce accountability. The From Retrieval to Reasoning paper examines how SOP accessibility shapes interpretation at the moment of action.
▸CAPACorrective and Preventive Action
Systematic process triggered after quality events to identify root causes and implement corrections. Must be documented, reviewed, and closed within defined timelines.
In this researchCAPA workflows are the empirical context for the Review Dashboard tool and the Friction Before Failure paper.
▸RCARoot Cause Analysis
Structured investigation method for identifying underlying causes of quality events. Must demonstrate systematic reasoning — not just proximate causes but contributing factors.
In this researchRCA processes are the site where premature accountability convergence occurs. The RCA Scaffolder introduces AI as "comparison enforcer" — preventing closure before alternatives are exhausted.
▸FDA Warning Letter
Official notification from the FDA to firms with significant regulatory violations. Public documents. Firms must respond within 15 business days with corrective actions.
In this researchA corpus of N=50 letters (160 violations, 2016–2025) serves as the empirical basis for the computational research program.
Research Concepts
▸Regulatory HCImy framework
A framework that reframes regulated environments — pharmaceutical manufacturing, clinical healthcare, aviation, financial compliance — as interaction systems rather than documentation systems. It identifies four interdependent layers (accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning) at which design choices determine accountability conditions for the people working inside the system.
Core claimThe same design choices that determine what workers can see, do, and decide also determine how responsibility gets produced and assigned. Improving the working environment and clarifying accountability structures are the same problem.
lineage:Star & Ruhleder (infrastructure) · Suchman (situated action) · Orlikowski (sociomateriality) · Amershi et al. (human-AI interaction)
▸Judgment Preservationmy design principle
Design principle for AI-integrated regulated work: systems should support practitioner judgment rather than replace it. AI functions as comparison enforcer, friction surface, and interpretive scaffold — not as attribution closer or decision maker.
Contrast withAutomation-focused AI design ("replace human decision-making") and explanation-focused AI design ("justify AI's decision").
operationalized in:Regulatory HCI four-layer framework
▸4-Stage Accountability LifecycleAAC → CF → RC → PAC
Theoretical framework tracing how responsibility is produced across time in regulated organizations. Four stages: AAC (Anticipated Accountability Convergence — how organizations structure responsibility before action), CF (Cognitive Friction — how procedural documents distribute interpretive burden), RC (Responsibility Convergence — how investigative systems stabilize attribution after events), and PAC (Premature Accountability Convergence — how AI accelerates closure of interpretive possibility).
StatusAll four stages currently under review at peer-reviewed organization theory journals.
Research Traditions
▸HCIHuman-Computer Interaction
Field studying how people interact with computational systems, combining user research, design, and empirical evaluation. Primary venues: CHI, CSCW, DIS, TOCHI.
In this researchHCI provides the design framework and empirical methods (N=20 think-aloud studies, prototype evaluation) for the tool deployment program.
▸STSScience and Technology Studies
Interdisciplinary field studying how scientific knowledge and technological systems are socially produced and embedded in organizations. Draws on ethnomethodology, actor-network theory, and infrastructure studies.
In this researchSTS provides the theoretical vocabulary for the 4-stage lifecycle and the conceptual framework for understanding accountability as produced rather than assigned.
