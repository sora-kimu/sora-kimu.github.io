Working thesis

Responsibility is produced, not assigned.

"Produced" means responsibility emerges through organizational processes — documentation regimes, investigative systems, attribution rituals, and increasingly the interfaces practitioners use — rather than pre-existing and merely labeled.

This shifts the design question from "who is responsible?" to "how does this system produce responsibility?" The first question takes accountability as given. The second treats it as a designed outcome — and therefore as something a designer can intervene in.

Theoretical lineage: Garfinkel · Star & Ruhleder · Suchman · Orlikowski · Amershi et al.

A note on framing The shift from assigned to produced is the move that turns accountability into a design problem.
Research program

Regulatory HCI — designing interfaces for work that gets audited.

In regulated environments — pharmaceutical manufacturing, aviation, clinical care, finance — every action a practitioner takes can later be reviewed by a regulator. That changes what interfaces are for: not just efficiency, but preserving the conditions of judgment — keeping reasoning legible, traceable, and open enough that the person using the tool can stand behind their decision.

How Regulated Systems Produce Responsibility
The framework. Four interaction layers, each grounded in a deployed prototype and an N=20 study.
Layer 4 Reasoning Investigation scaffolder

During root-cause investigations, the tool detects when reasoning is narrowing too early and reopens alternatives. AI as comparison enforcer, not explanation generator. The most direct attempt at preserving judgment in the stack.

Paper: Keeping Comparison OpenMethod: N=20 think-aloud
Layer 3 Coordination Review dashboard

Makes the invisible review burden across corrective-action workflows (CAPA) visible to managers and reviewers. Surfaces friction before it becomes a failure to investigate. Accountability labor stops being hidden.

Paper: Friction Before FailureMethod: Behavior log + N=20 study
Layer 2 Structure Document traceability · Navigation tracker

Surfaces the chain of references between regulatory documents that practitioners normally reconstruct by hand. The tracker logs how analysts actually navigate during real investigations — empirical input for the rest of the design papers.

Paper: When Documents Don't ConnectMethod: Behavior log + N=20 study
Layer 1 Accessibility Procedure retrieval

Semantic search over the regulated procedures (SOPs) practitioners must follow at every step. Reframes retrieval as an interpretive act, not just access — what you can reach is what you can reason about.

Paper: From Retrieval to ReasoningMethod: Task-based study, N=20

A second method: computational analysis of regulatory text
A complementary thread studies the same convergence at the institutional scale — how FDA Warning Letters and NTSB accident findings structurally produce attribution. The same closure mechanism, in opposite institutional directions.

Why two threads The same closure mechanism shows up at both scales — at the desk of one analyst, and across 50 FDA Warning Letters.
