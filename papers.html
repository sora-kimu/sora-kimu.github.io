STS & Organizational Theory

A four-stage lifecycle: how responsibility gets structured before action (AAC), distributed through documents (CF), stabilized through investigation (RC), and accelerated by AI (PAC). All four under review at organization theory journals.

STS · Organizational Theory · AAC
Anticipated Accountability Convergence: How Organizations Structure Responsibility Before Action
Under review
Introduces "Anticipated Accountability Convergence" (AAC) as the pre-failure structuring of responsibility through three structural components: procedural structuring, documentation regimes, and evaluative anticipation. Develops four propositions grounded in Garfinkel, Latour, and Star & Ruhleder.

STS · Document Infrastructure · CF
Cognitive Friction: Procedural Documents as Accountability Infrastructure in Regulated Organizations
Under review
Operationalizes cognitive friction at sentence level across a 400-sentence corpus from regulated procedural documents. Identifies conditional branching as the primary source of friction. Reframes procedural friction not as cognitive burden but as structural condition shaping accountability.

STS · Investigative Systems · RC
Responsibility Convergence: How Investigative Systems Stabilize Attribution
Under review
Identifies three mechanisms through which investigative systems converge on firm-level attribution: ambiguity resolution, closure pressure, and legibility bias. Empirical anchor: 159/160 FDA Warning Letters converge on firm-level responsibility.

STS · AI & Technology · PAC
Premature Accountability Convergence: How Technology Accelerates Responsibility Stabilization in Organizations
Under review
Reframes AI's role from "acceleration" to "earlier institutional closure of interpretive possibility." Three mechanisms: representational compression, epistemic authority amplification, and temporal contraction. Distinguishes PAC from Orlikowski/Leonardi sociomateriality.

HCI & Design Research

Five studies, each grounded in a deployed prototype with N=20 GMP-experienced participants. Together they articulate the Regulatory HCI framework — four interdependent layers (accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning) at which design choices both improve practitioner working conditions and clarify accountability structures.

HCI · Framework · Umbrella
How Regulated Systems Produce Responsibility: A Four-Layer Framework for Interaction Design
Working paper
The Regulatory HCI framework — reframing regulated environments as interaction systems rather than documentation systems. Four interdependent layers (accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning) at which design choices determine both worker cognition and accountability conditions. Three design principles: upstream stabilization, observability over optimization, and judgment preservation.

HCI · Layer 2 · Structure · Cross-Document Coordination
When Documents Don't Connect: Traceability Burden and Loopback Behavior in GMP Document Navigation
Working paper
Identifies the human as "missing infrastructure" in cross-document accountability gaps. Two-study paper: (1) NetworkX-based directed graph analysis surfacing 31 missing CAPA links, with a traceability visibility prototype reducing path reconstruction time from ~7 to ~4 minutes; (2) behavioral navigation tracker capturing loopback events as evidence of unresolved judgment demand. Working paper

HCI · Layer 4 · Reasoning · AI Scaffolding
Keeping Comparison Open: Interface Scaffolding Against Premature Accountability Convergence in Regulated Investigations
Working paper
Introduces AI as comparison enforcer — detecting premature closure and reopening the comparison space, rather than generating candidate explanations. Two-study design (N=20 each): timed reflection protocol at three timepoints (15/20 single-cause attributions at t=0; 18/20 broadened by t=+10min) + AI-supported reasoning scaffold organized into five stages enforcing explicit comparison before closure.

Computational Research

A unified computational framework integrating four signal layers — detection, graph structure, attribution, distribution — applied to FDA Warning Letters. External validation of the lifecycle theory at scale, with cross-framework convergence around WL2018-05 as extreme outlier.

Computational · Umbrella · RCI
Responsibility as Multi-Layer Signal: A Computational Framework for FDA Warning Letters
Working paper

Integrates four computational signal layers into the Responsibility Convergence Index (RCI) via PCA compression and Ridge Regression weight learning (w_ps=0.995). Empirical corpus: FDA Warning Letters (N=50, 160 violations, 2016–2025, 21 CFR 210/211). Five cross-framework findings include structural pre-convergence in FDA Warning Letters (Firm attribution dominates 99% due to enforcement document logic) and WL2018-05 as extreme convergence outlier across all four signal layers independently.

Four integrated framework components:

  • ⚖️ PAC Detection — sentence-level detection of premature accountability convergence in regulatory investigative texts (F1 0.901–0.913). Identifies structural pre-convergence as a measurable property of enforcement documents.
  • 🕸️ Accountability Graph Extraction — directed graph representations of attribution structure across FDA Warning Letters. Reveals star topology centered on "Firm" (in-degree 0.444), with System, Individual, and Material entities occupying peripheral positions despite being causally implicated.
  • 🧪 Attribution Prediction — classification approach predicting attribution targets from violation text. Logistic Regression F1=0.725, Individual AUC=0.940. When corrected for text-surface bias, 30.6% of firm-labeled cases reassign to System.
  • 📈 Responsibility Distribution Metric (RDM) — composite metric combining entropy and centrality measures of attribution distribution. Cross-framework convergence validates WL2018-05 as extreme outlier across all weight schemes.

Given the use of theoretically-motivated synthetic labels, this framework is presented as a proof-of-concept operationalization rather than a validated detection system.

📖 Glossary
Terms used across this research
GMP Practice
▸GMPGood Manufacturing Practice
FDA-enforced standards for pharmaceutical production. Covers documentation, personnel training, facility design, equipment qualification, and quality systems.
In this researchGMP serves as the empirical site for studying how accountability structures are produced in regulated work.
▸SOPStandard Operating Procedure
Formal documents specifying step-by-step procedures for regulated activities. Following an SOP is both operational guidance and legal protection.
In this researchSOPs are studied as part of the structural conditions that shape interpretation and produce accountability — the From Retrieval to Reasoning paper examines how accessibility shapes interpretation at the moment of action.
▸CAPACorrective and Preventive Action
Systematic process triggered after quality events to identify root causes and implement corrections.
In this researchCAPA workflows are the empirical context for the Review Dashboard and Friction Before Failure.
▸RCARoot Cause Analysis
Structured investigation method for identifying underlying causes of quality events. Must demonstrate systematic reasoning beyond proximate causes.
In this researchRCA is the site where premature accountability convergence occurs — the RCA Scaffolder introduces AI as "comparison enforcer."
▸FDA Warning Letter
Official notification from the FDA to firms with significant regulatory violations. Public documents. Firms must respond within 15 business days.
In this researchN=50 letters (160 violations, 2016–2025) serve as the empirical basis for the computational program.
Research Concepts
▸Regulatory HCImy framework
A framework that reframes regulated environments as interaction systems rather than documentation systems. Identifies four interdependent layers (accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning) at which design choices both improve practitioner working conditions and clarify accountability structures.
Core claimThe same design choices that determine what workers can see, do, and decide also determine how responsibility gets produced. Improving the working environment and clarifying accountability are the same problem.
lineage:Star & Ruhleder · Suchman · Orlikowski · Amershi et al. (human-AI interaction)
▸Judgment Preservationmy design principle
AI should support practitioner judgment rather than replace it — functioning as comparison enforcer, friction surface, and interpretive scaffold.
Contrast withAutomation-focused design ("replace decision-making") and explanation-focused design ("justify AI's decision").
▸4-Stage Accountability LifecycleAAC → CF → RC → PAC
Theoretical framework tracing how responsibility is produced across time: AAC (Anticipated Accountability Convergence — structured before action), CF (Cognitive Friction — distributed through procedural documents), RC (Responsibility Convergence — stabilized through investigation), and PAC (Premature Accountability Convergence — accelerated by AI).
StatusAll four stages currently under review at peer-reviewed organization theory journals.
Research Traditions
▸HCIHuman-Computer Interaction
Field studying how people interact with computational systems, combining user research, design, and empirical evaluation. Primary venues: CHI, CSCW, DIS, TOCHI.
In this researchHCI provides the design framework and empirical methods (N=20 think-aloud studies, prototype evaluation) for the tool deployment program.
▸STSScience and Technology Studies
Interdisciplinary field studying how scientific knowledge and technological systems are socially produced and embedded in organizations. Draws on ethnomethodology, actor-network theory, and infrastructure studies.
In this researchSTS provides the theoretical vocabulary for the 4-stage lifecycle and the conceptual framework for understanding accountability as produced rather than assigned.
