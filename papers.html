Design research

Five papers on AI tools deployed in pharmaceutical quality control workflows. One framework paper plus four interaction-layer studies, each with an N=20 user study, organized from accessibility (Layer 1) up to reasoning (Layer 4).

Framework · 2026

Accountability Infrastructure Design: A Four-Layer Framework for Judgment-Preserving Systems in Regulated Work

AI as an external layer on validated regulatory systems — designed across four interaction layers (accessibility → reasoning) to support, not substitute, practitioner judgment.

Framework paper·Synthesizes 4 layer studies (N=20 each)·GMP pharmaceutical QC

Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Regulated environments — pharmaceutical manufacturing, aviation, clinical care — share a common interaction problem: every action a practitioner takes is auditable, and AI tools must be designed to fit inside that constraint rather than around it. I propose a four-layer framework for designing AI as an external layer on validated regulatory systems, organized by the level of practitioner work each layer intervenes in: accessibility (what information can be reached), structure (how documents connect), coordination (how review work is distributed), and reasoning (how investigations are conducted). At each layer, the design principle is the same: AI should make work more legible and reduce systemic friction, without replacing the judgment that the audit trail is meant to preserve. Four interaction-layer studies (N=20 each) deployed in a GMP pharmaceutical quality lab instantiate the framework.

Layer 4 · Reasoning · 2026

Keeping Comparison Open: Interface Scaffolding Against Premature Accountability Convergence in Regulated Investigations

AI as comparison enforcer, not explanation generator — detects when reasoning narrows too early during RCA and reopens alternative hypotheses.

N=20 think-aloud·Real deviation cases·GMP RCA workflow

Request full paper ↗
Abstract

In root-cause analysis (RCA) within regulated manufacturing, investigative reasoning often closes prematurely on a single explanation despite available alternatives — a pattern I call premature accountability convergence. I designed and deployed an investigation scaffolder that intervenes at the reasoning layer: rather than generating explanations for the analyst to accept, the tool detects narrowing in the analyst's own reasoning trajectory and surfaces alternative hypotheses still consistent with the evidence. In a think-aloud study with twenty QC analysts working on real deviation cases, the scaffolder shifted the role of AI from answer provider to comparison enforcer: participants generated more alternative explanations before committing to a root cause, without reduction in investigation efficiency. The paper argues that judgment-preserving AI in safety-critical work is not about constraining the system's confidence, but about designing for the user's own comparison process.

Layer 3 · Coordination · 2026

Friction Before Failure: Making Review Burden Visible in Regulated Document Work

Make review burden visible before it becomes a missed deadline — without changing what the regulations require.

Behavior log + N=20 study·Managers and reviewers·CAPA workflows

Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Corrective and preventive action (CAPA) workflows in regulated manufacturing distribute review responsibility across multiple reviewers, deadlines, and document versions. The resulting review burden is invisible to the people coordinating it — until a deadline slips and the failure becomes a formal deviation. I designed a review dashboard that makes the friction visible before it becomes failure: surfacing where reviews are queued, how long they've been waiting, and which reviewers are overloaded. Behavior log analysis combined with an N=20 study with managers and reviewers shows that the dashboard does not change what regulations require, but redistributes attention earlier in the workflow.

Layer 2 · Structure · 2026

When Documents Don't Connect: Traceability Burden and Loopback Behavior in GMP Document Navigation

Surface the chain of document references analysts reconstruct by hand — and quantify the loopback behavior that hidden structure creates.

Behavior log + N=20 study·Real investigations·GMP document corpus

Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Regulated documents — SOPs, batch records, deviation reports, change controls — reference each other in chains that practitioners must reconstruct by hand during investigations. I deployed two tools at the structure layer: a traceability viewer that surfaces inter-document references as a navigable graph, and a navigation tracker that logs how analysts actually move through documents during real investigations. The tracker reveals a frequent loopback pattern: analysts return to the same document multiple times, often re-reading the same passage, because the connection they need was never made explicit. Combining behavior-log data with an N=20 task study, the paper characterizes the traceability burden as a structural property of the document system, not an individual skill gap.

Layer 1 · Accessibility · 2026

From Retrieval to Reasoning: How SOP Accessibility Shapes Interpretation in Regulated Work

Retrieval is an interpretive act, not a precursor — what counts as "relevant" depends on how the analyst is already framing the situation.

Task-based study, N=20·Semantic search over SOPs·GMP QC analysts

Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Standard operating procedures (SOPs) are the regulated text practitioners must follow at every step — but only what they can reach is what they can reason about. I deployed a semantic search tool over a GMP pharmaceutical site's SOP corpus and conducted a task-based study (N=20) in which QC analysts located the procedures relevant to realistic work scenarios. The paper reframes retrieval as an interpretive act, not a precursor to it: what counts as a "relevant" SOP depends on how the analyst is framing the situation, and the search interface itself shapes that framing. Findings show that semantic search outperforms keyword search on accessibility, but the more consequential design choice is what the interface tells the analyst about why a result was retrieved.

Computational research on regulatory text

Five papers on a computational framework for analyzing how accountability is constructed in regulatory enforcement text. Empirical site: FDA Warning Letters corpus (N=50, 160 violations, 2016–2025, 21 CFR 210/211).

Framework · 2026

Responsibility as a Multi-Layer Signal: A Unified Computational Framework for Accountability Convergence in Regulatory Investigations

Integrates process, structural, and linguistic signals into a composite Responsibility Convergence Index via PCA + Ridge Regression
Process-structural signal dominates: w = 0.995
WL2018-05 identified as extreme convergence case across all four frameworks independently
Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Responsibility in regulatory investigations is not a single label — it is a multi-layered phenomenon produced through language, relational structure, and investigative process simultaneously. I present a proof-of-concept computational framework that operationalizes accountability convergence across three representational layers: process-level premature convergence detection, structural accountability graph extraction, and linguistic responsibility attribution prediction. These three frameworks are integrated into a composite Responsibility Convergence Index (RCI) through PCA compression and Ridge Regression weight learning. Applied to an FDA Warning Letter corpus (N=50, 160 violations, 2016–2025, 21 CFR 210/211), the unified framework produces five cross-framework findings: (1) process-structural signals dominate accountability convergence in enforcement documents (w = 0.995); (2) linguistic attribution is the most individually distinct signal (Individual AUC = 0.940) but contributes minimally to composite convergence; (3) WL2018-05 is identified as the extreme convergence case across all four frameworks independently; (4) FDA Warning Letters exhibit structural pre-convergence in which Firm attribution dominates (99%) due to enforcement document logic rather than investigative reasoning; and (5) accountability convergence is weight-invariant at its extremes.

Process-level · 2026

Detecting Premature Accountability Convergence: A Computational Framework for Regulatory Investigations

Operationalizes PAC as a process-quality detection task, distinct from correctness assessment
Synthetic dataset n = 500 validated against FDA WL corpus; AUC > 0.97 across three classifiers
Strongest predictor: commitment level (ΔF1 = 0.060); SHAP attributes case variance to explanation count
Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Premature accountability convergence (PAC) occurs when investigative reasoning closes too early on a single explanation, despite available alternatives. While prior work has theorized this phenomenon in regulated environments, no computational framework exists for its detection. I present a feature-based classification approach that operationalizes PAC as a process-quality detection task, distinct from correctness assessment. Using a synthetic dataset (n=500) validated against an FDA Warning Letter corpus (N=50, 2016–2025), I train and evaluate three classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM), achieving AUC scores above 0.97 across all models. Ablation analysis identifies commitment level as the strongest predictor (ΔF1 = 0.060), while SHAP analysis reveals that explanation count drives case-level variation.

Structural · 2026

Extracting Accountability Graphs from Regulatory Text: A Structural Analysis of the FDA Warning Letter Corpus

Extracts 320 accountability triples across 3 relation types (VIOLATES, INVOLVES, ATTRIBUTED_TO)
Star-topology structure: Firm dominates as central node, in-degree centrality = 0.444
Openness score correlates negatively with PAC risk: r = −0.431, p = 0.0018
Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Accountability in regulatory investigations is not merely a legal designation — it is a relational structure produced through documentation practices. I present a graph-based framework for extracting accountability relationships from regulatory text and representing them as directed graphs. Applied to an FDA Warning Letter corpus (N=50, 2016–2025, 21 CFR 210/211), the framework extracts 320 accountability triples across three relation types (VIOLATES, INVOLVES, ATTRIBUTED_TO), constructs case-level accountability graphs, and computes structural metrics including centrality, openness score, and cluster membership. Graph analysis reveals a star-topology accountability structure with Firm as the dominant node (in-degree centrality = 0.444). A composite accountability openness score exhibits significant negative correlation with premature convergence risk (Pearson r = −0.431, p = 0.0018; Spearman ρ = −0.742, p < 0.001), providing cross-framework validation. Cases cluster into three structural types: Open, Standard, and Extreme convergence.

Linguistic · 2026

Responsibility Attribution Prediction from Regulatory Text: A Linguistic Analysis of Accountability Construction

Predicts attribution (Firm / Individual / System) from violation text alone: macro F1 = 0.725
Individual attribution most distinct: AUC = 0.940; model reassigns 49 of 160 Firm-labeled violations to System
LIME reveals class-specific markers: Firm = inadequate, failure to establish; Individual = operator, analyst; System = equipment, malfunction
Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Responsibility attribution in regulatory investigations is not discovered from evidence — it is linguistically constructed through word choice, syntactic framing, and discourse patterns. I present a text-based classification framework that predicts responsibility attribution (Firm, Individual, System) from violation text alone, using TF-IDF features and Logistic Regression. Applied to an annotated corpus of GMP regulatory language (n=45) with Leave-One-Out cross-validation, the framework achieves macro F1 = 0.725 and demonstrates that Individual-level attribution is most linguistically distinct (AUC = 0.940), while System-level attribution exhibits the lowest discriminability (AUC = 0.764). When applied to the FDA Warning Letter corpus (N=50, 160 violations, 2016–2025), the model reassigns 49 of 160 Firm-labeled violations to System, suggesting that regulatory enforcement documents systematically attribute infrastructure failures to organizational responsibility. LIME analysis reveals class-specific linguistic markers: Firm attribution is signaled through systemic failure language (inadequate, failure to establish), Individual through agent-action language (operator, analyst), and System through infrastructure language (equipment, malfunction).

Composite · 2026

Responsibility Distribution Metric: A Multi-Layer Computational Index for Accountability Concentration in Regulatory Investigations

Composite RCI metric unifying three frameworks via PCA + Ridge Regression (PC1 = 98.6% variance)
Empirical finding: accountability concentration is primarily structural, not linguistic (w = 0.995)
Case rankings robust across four weight schemes: ρ > 0.97 for non-Ridge schemes
Request full paper ↗
Abstract

Responsibility in regulatory investigations is not a single label — it is a multi-layered signal detectable at the linguistic, structural, and process level simultaneously. I present the Responsibility Concentration Index (RCI), a composite metric that unifies three independent computational frameworks into a single accountability concentration score. Applied to an FDA Warning Letter corpus (N=50, 160 violations, 2016–2025), RCI is derived from process-level PAC risk scores, structural centrality measures, and linguistic attribution entropy, with weights learned via Ridge Regression following PCA compression of correlated signals (PC1 = 98.6% variance explained). The learned weight structure reveals that process-structural signal dominates RCI (w = 0.995), reflecting a corpus-level finding: in FDA Warning Letters, accountability concentration is primarily a structural phenomenon, not a linguistic one.
