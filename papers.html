Under Review

A four-paper lifecycle theory of accountability in regulated organizations: pre-action structuring (AAC), procedural document infrastructure (CF), investigative convergence (RC), and AI-accelerated closure (PAC). The lifecycle articulates how responsibility is produced structurally at each stage, with implications for AI integration in regulated work.

STS · Organizational Theory · AAC
Accountability Before Action: How Organizations Structure Responsibility in Advance of Outcomes
Under review · Academy of Management Review (AMR-2026-0516)
Introduces "Accountability Before Action" (AAC) as the pre-failure structuring of responsibility through three structural components: procedural structuring, documentation regimes, and evaluative anticipation. Develops four propositions grounded in Garfinkel, Latour, and Star & Ruhleder.
📄 SSRN
⚖️ AAC 🔍 Infrastructure studies 📐 Organizational theory 🏭 Regulated work
STS · Document Infrastructure · CF
Cognitive Friction: Procedural Documents as Accountability Infrastructure in Regulated Organizations
Under review · Organization (ORG-26-0241)
Operationalizes cognitive friction at sentence level across a 400-sentence corpus from regulated procedural documents. Identifies conditional branching as the primary source of friction. Reframes procedural friction not as cognitive burden but as accountability infrastructure.
📄 SSRN
📄 CF 🔍 Document infrastructure 📊 Corpus analysis
STS · Investigative Systems · RC
Responsibility Convergence: How Investigative Systems Stabilize Attribution
Under review · Organization Science (ORSC-MS-2026-22511)
Identifies three mechanisms through which investigative systems converge on firm-level attribution: ambiguity resolution, closure pressure, and legibility bias. Four propositions. Empirical anchor: FDA Warning Letters show 159/160 cases converge on firm-level responsibility.
📄 SSRN
⚖️ RC 📄 FDA Warning Letters 🔍 Attribution
STS · AI & Technology · PAC
Premature Accountability Convergence: How Technology Accelerates Responsibility Stabilization in Organizations
Under review · Information and Organization (INFORG-D-26-00222)
Reframes AI's role from "acceleration" to "earlier institutional closure of interpretive possibility." Three mechanisms: representational compression, epistemic authority amplification, and temporal contraction. Distinguishes PAC from Orlikowski/Leonardi sociomateriality.
📄 SSRN
🤖 PAC ⏱️ Temporal closure 🏭 Sociomateriality

HCI & Design Research

Five HCI papers on judgment-preserving design in regulated work, each grounded in deployed production tools. Together they articulate a four-layer framework — accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning — for accountability infrastructure design in AI-integrated regulated environments. N=20 user studies.

HCI · Framework · TOCHI
Accountability Infrastructure Design: A Four-Layer Framework for Judgment-Preserving Systems in Regulated Work
In preparation · ACM TOCHI
Umbrella paper synthesizing five HCI studies. Introduces a four-layer framework (accessibility, structure, coordination, reasoning) and three design principles: upstream stabilization, observability over optimization, and judgment preservation.

From Retrieval to Reasoning: How SOP Accessibility Shapes Interpretation in Regulated Work
Working paper · SSRN

HCI · Layer 2 · Structure · Cross-Document Coordination
When Documents Don't Connect: Traceability Burden and Loopback Behavior in GMP Document Navigation
Working paper · SSRN
Identifies the human as "missing infrastructure" in cross-document accountability gaps. Grounded in a two-component deployed system: a traceability layer that surfaces cross-document links, and a behavioral tracker that captures loopback patterns. Loopback as method. Direct Star & Ruhleder lineage on the social organization of infrastructure.

Computational Research

A computational research program operationalizing accountability across four signal layers (detection, graph structure, attribution, distribution), applied to FDA Warning Letters and synthesized in the umbrella paper. These analyses provide external validation of the lifecycle theory through large-scale regulatory text. Cross-framework convergent validity around WL2018-05.

Computational · Umbrella
Responsibility as Multi-Layer Signal: A Computational Framework for FDA Warning Letters
Working paper · SSRN · Umbrella
Integrates PAC Detection (F1 0.901–0.913), Accountability Graph Extraction (star topology, Firm in-degree 0.444), Attribution Prediction (LR F1=0.725, Individual AUC=0.940), and RDM (PCA+Ridge, w_ps=0.995) into the Responsibility Convergence Index (RCI).
📧 Available upon request
📊 Computational accountability 📄 FDA Warning Letters 🕸️ Multi-layer signal
Computational · NLP · Detection
Premature Convergence Detection in Regulatory Investigative Texts
Working paper · SSRN
Detects structural pre-convergence in FDA Warning Letters (F1 0.901–0.913). WL2018-05 emerges as an extreme outlier, flagged by all four frameworks.
🐙 GitHub 📧 Available upon request

Computational · Graph Extraction
Accountability Graph Extraction from Regulatory Texts
Working paper · SSRN
Extracts accountability graphs from FDA Warning Letters. Reveals star topology centered on "Firm" (in-degree 0.444) as structural pattern. System, Individual, and Material entities occupy peripheral positions despite being causally implicated.

Responsibility Attribution Prediction: A Classification Approach to FDA Warning Letters
Working paper · SSRN
Classification approach to attribution prediction: Individual AUC=0.940; Logistic Regression F1=0.725. When corrected for text-surface bias, 30.6% of cases reassign from Firm to System.

Working paper · SSRN
