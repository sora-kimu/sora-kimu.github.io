Papers

Seven papers across two tracks

Four organizational-theory and STS papers develop a lifecycle of how responsibility is structured before action, accumulated in procedural documents, stabilized through investigation, and accelerated by AI. Three computational social science papers operationalize that lifecycle across regulatory corpora, with cross-domain validation between pharmaceutical and aviation.

— Organizational theory & STS · 4 papers

A lifecycle of accountability in regulated organizations

Four papers covering the full lifecycle: pre-action structuring (AAC), procedural document infrastructure (CF), investigative convergence (RC), and AI-accelerated closure (PAC). Each can be read independently; together they form a single arc.

Paper 1 · AAC Working manuscript

Anticipated Accountability Convergence: How Organizations Structure Responsibility Before Action

Theorizes how organizations structure responsibility before action occurs — through role definitions, procedural anticipation, and pre-allocated accountability slots. The first stage of the lifecycle: accountability begins as anticipation, embedded in organizational forms long before any specific event triggers it.

Paper 2 · CF Working manuscript

Cognitive Friction: Procedural Documents as Accountability Infrastructure in Regulated Organizations

Argues that procedural documents — SOPs, work instructions, deviation protocols — are not neutral records but accountability infrastructure. Their friction (length, fragmentation, retrieval cost) is itself a coordination signal: an organizational technology for distributing where responsibility settles when something goes wrong.

Paper 3 · RC Working manuscript

Responsibility Convergence: How Investigative Systems Stabilize Attribution

Examines how investigative processes — root cause analysis, accident inquiries, regulatory enforcement — actively converge on a stable attribution of responsibility. Convergence is not discovery; it is production. The investigation shapes the answer it appears to find, often under closure pressure that runs faster than the evidence supports.

📄 Request PDF
Paper 4 · PAC Working manuscript

Premature Accountability Convergence: How Technology Accelerates Responsibility Stabilization in Organizations

Extends RC into the AI era. As organizations adopt language models and automated decision tools, accountability stabilizes faster than the investigation requires — sometimes faster than the evidence permits. The result is premature convergence: explanations that close before alternatives are fully ruled out, with technology providing the artifact that justifies closure.

— Computational social science · 3 papers

Operationalizing the lifecycle across regulatory domains

The theoretical lifecycle, made testable. FDA Warning Letters (N=50, 160 violations) and NTSB aviation investigations (n=71,114 findings, 23,742 narratives) serve as methodologically contrasting corpora — pharmaceutical regulation makes only final outputs public; aviation regulation makes the entire investigative process traceable. The contrast itself is what validates the theory.

Paper 5 · FDA Working manuscript

Responsibility as Multi-Layer Signal: A Computational Framework for FDA Warning Letters

Synthesizes four computational components — premature-convergence detection, accountability-graph extraction, responsibility-attribution prediction, and a responsibility-distribution metric — into a unified framework for analyzing FDA Warning Letters as a regulatory enforcement corpus. Demonstrates the lifecycle theory empirically on pharmaceutical regulation's most consequential public output.

Paper 6 · NTSB Working manuscript

Designed Attribution: Coding-Level and Grammatical-Level Evidence of Organizational Accountability Displacement in NTSB Aviation Investigations

Two-phase analysis of the full NTSB Aviation Accident Database (1982–2025): structured coding of n=71,114 findings, plus dependency-parsed probable cause statements (n=23,742). Triangulated finding: organizational accountability appears in only 0.42–0.58% of NTSB outputs across both methods — a designed pattern of attribution displacement, not a measurement artifact.

Paper 7 · FDA vs NTSB In preparation

FDA vs NTSB: Cross-Domain Validation of the Convergence Lifecycle

A direct comparison paper using both corpora as cross-domain validation. Pharmaceutical regulation (FDA Warning Letters) and aviation regulation (NTSB investigations) operate under different visibility regimes and produce different artifacts — yet the same lifecycle of accountability convergence appears in both. The contrast strengthens the theoretical claim: convergence is a property of regulated investigation itself, not an artifact of either domain.

— Research corpora & data infrastructure

What the papers are built on

The empirical foundation: regulatory corpora constructed from public data, plus a four-year field observation archive from inside a GMP-regulated pharmaceutical site.

FDA Warning Letters Corpus
N = 50 letters · 160 violations · 2016–2025

FDA's formal regulatory enforcement documents — issued when significant compliance failures are identified. The pharmaceutical regulator's most consequential public output. Used across the organizational theory, STS, and computational papers.

NTSB Aviation Accident Corpus
71,114 findings · 27,877 narratives · 1982–2025

Full Aviation Accident Database (avall.mdb, ~546 MB) analyzed directly. Methodological contrast with FDA: aviation regulation makes the entire investigative pipeline traceable, whereas FDA makes only final outputs public. The cross-domain comparison provides the strongest external validation site for the lifecycle theory.

Cognitive Friction Corpus
400 sentences · sentence-level annotation

400 naturally occurring sentences from regulated procedural documents, manually annotated at sentence level for cognitive friction features. Empirical base for the CF paper.

Field Observation Archive
~4 years · embedded ethnography

Field notes from inside a GMP-regulated pharmaceutical site (September 2022 – present). The ethnographic ground that the theoretical papers rest on, and the empirical substrate for the design research on the Tools page.
