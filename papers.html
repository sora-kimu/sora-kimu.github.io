Keeping Comparison Open: Interface Scaffolding Against Premature Accountability Convergence in Regulated Investigations
Problem Root Cause Analysis (RCA) in regulated environments collapses comparison before alternatives are meaningfully examined — not because investigators lack reasoning ability, but because the workflow structurally forecloses it.
Approach Two-study design: Study 1 establishes the behavioral baseline using a timed reflection protocol; Study 2 introduces an AI-supported RCA Reasoning Scaffolder that detects convergence signals and reopens the comparison space.
Takeaway AI in investigation support should function as a comparison enforcer — resisting premature closure — not as an explanation source that accelerates it.

How Regulated Systems Produce Responsibility: A Four-Layer Framework for Interaction Design
Problem Regulated environments are treated as documentation systems — whether records are accurate and audits pass. This misses how design actively shapes how practitioners interpret situations, make decisions, and assign responsibility.
Approach The Regulatory HCI framework identifies four interdependent layers — accessibility, structure, coordination, and reasoning — grounded in four prototype-based empirical studies (N=20 each).
Takeaway Responsibility is not only assigned after the fact — it is structured in advance by system design. Compliance-constrained design is accountability production.

When Documents Don't Connect: Traceability Burden and Loopback Behavior in GMP Document Navigation
Problem In Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) environments, documents form interdependent networks — yet cross-references are textual rather than navigable. When links break, practitioners become the missing infrastructure.
Approach Two studies: a graph-based Traceability Explorer that makes document linkage navigable, and a Cognitive Navigation Explorer that operationalizes loopback behavior as an empirical indicator of structural friction.
Takeaway Navigation burden falls hardest on those with least experience — not because they reason poorly, but because experienced practitioners carry an internalized map the system never provides.

Friction Before Failure: Making Review Burden Visible in Regulated Document Work
Problem Deviation and Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) review workflows accumulate coordination burden invisibly — no single participant sees the full pattern, and delay gets attributed to individuals rather than structure.
Approach A six-tab workflow dashboard combined with a TF-IDF pattern detection layer surfaces hidden coordination work as interpretable signal, without modifying validated systems or automating quality decisions.
Takeaway Making friction visible doesn't eliminate it — but it makes the structural conditions that produce delay discussable, and shifts accountability away from individual blame.

From Retrieval to Reasoning: How SOP Accessibility Shapes Interpretation in Regulated Work
Problem Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are legally binding — but their practical force depends on whether practitioners can reach them at the moment action is required. Poor retrieval shapes what gets considered, justified, and done.
Approach An external keyword-searchable retrieval layer over a synthetic corpus of 500 GMP SOPs, evaluated using a think-aloud protocol with N=20 frequent SOP users.
Takeaway Retrieval difficulty leads practitioners to act within a personally known subset of the archive — not non-compliance, but a rational response to a system that fails to externalize its own structure.

Anticipated Accountability Convergence: Structuring Responsibility Before Action
Problem Existing accounts treat accountability as retrospective — assigned after outcomes occur. But in regulated environments, responsibility expectations shape how actors interpret obligations and risks before any action unfolds.
Approach Introduces Anticipated Accountability Convergence (AAC) as a process-level framework with three structural components — procedural structuring, documentation regimes, and evaluative anticipation — and four theoretical propositions.
Takeaway Responsibility is built into the conditions of work in advance. Understanding anticipatory convergence is prerequisite to designing systems that can intervene in it.

Structuring Judgment Under Constraint: Cognitive Friction in Procedural Texts
Problem Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) don't merely instruct — they actively structure how actors interpret situations and allocate responsibility during execution. But the mechanism through which this happens has not been specified.
Approach Introduces cognitive friction as a document-level property, operationalized through rule-based computational annotation of conditional branching, cross-references, exception structures, and discretionary qualifiers across 500 procedural sentences.
Takeaway Interpretive difficulty in procedural work is structurally produced by document design, not individual cognition. The locus of intervention should shift from actor training to document architecture.

Responsibility Convergence in Organizational Investigation
Problem Investigative frameworks like Root Cause Analysis (RCA) are designed to diffuse responsibility across sociotechnical systems — yet they recurrently stabilize accountability at identifiable individuals, independent of investigator intent.
Approach Introduces responsibility convergence as a mechanism model with three interacting components: procedural ambiguity, attributional closure pressure, and accountability legibility bias.
Takeaway Blame concentration is a predictable structural outcome of how accountability infrastructures are designed — not a failure of investigative methodology or individual reasoning.

Premature Accountability Convergence in Regulated Organizations
Problem As AI systems become embedded in documentation and evaluative infrastructures, pre-existing responsibility convergence dynamics don't disappear — they accelerate. The temporal architecture of accountability shifts.
Approach Introduces PAC as a conditional acceleration dynamic driven by representational compression, epistemic authority amplification, and temporal contraction. Document analysis of FDA Warning Letters (N=50, 160 violations) + two empirical studies (N=20 each).
Takeaway AI accountability is not only about who is responsible — it is about how AI reshapes the timing, breadth, and durability of responsibility convergence. Counter-convergence design can resist this acceleration.

Responsibility as a Multi-Layer Signal: A Unified Computational Framework for Accountability Convergence in Regulatory Investigations
Problem Accountability in regulatory investigations is typically measured as a single outcome label. But responsibility is a multi-layered signal — detectable at the linguistic, structural, and process level simultaneously.
Method Three independently validated frameworks — TF-IDF text classification for attribution prediction, directed graph extraction for structural centrality analysis, and rule-based PAC detection — are integrated into a composite Responsibility Convergence Index via PCA compression and Ridge Regression weight learning. Applied to FDA Warning Letters (N=50, 160 violations, 2016–2025, 21 CFR 210/211).
Takeaway FDA Warning Letters exhibit structural pre-convergence: firm-level attribution is encoded in document architecture before investigative analysis begins. AI systems trained on such documents will inherit and amplify this pattern unless it is explicitly modeled as a corpus artifact.
