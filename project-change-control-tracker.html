Change Control Tracker

The validated system shows the team a list of change control numbers — no titles, no due dates, no visible structure of the actions inside each one. This tool surfaces what the validated system hides.

The problem

The validated system shows numbers. It doesn't show what they're for.

Change controls in regulated manufacturing live inside a validated electronic system. Every change is documented, reviewed, approved, and closed there. That system is necessary — it's the source of record. It is also not optimized for the question the team needs to answer every morning.

When the team opens the validated system, what they see is a list of change control numbers. No titles. No due dates. No visible structure of the actions inside each one. To know which change control is which, you click into them one by one. People who have opened a lot of change controls have to click through each one to remember which is which.

The structure underneath is the actual problem. A single change control contains multiple change actions — typically a document-revision action and a training action, sometimes more — and each change action carries its own deadline, its own status, its own extension history. When a change action is extended, it gets a new status downstream. The result is that one change control can quietly accumulate four or five different status states across its actions, and tracking which is what becomes a small forensic exercise the team performs in private.

In the validated system

A list of change control numbers. No titles. No due dates. To know what each number refers to, you click into it. Multiple actions inside each one, each with its own status and extension history — all hidden until you open them.

In this tool

Number, title, and earliest due date — all at the top level. Every change action inside each change control surfaced as its own row, with its own status, extension count, and deadline. Nothing collapsed, nothing hidden.

What it does

A layer that surfaces what the validated system collapses.

Title + number, at the top level
No more clicking through every change control to remember which is which. Title, scope, and current state are visible from the list view.
Every change action visible
Each change action appears as its own row under its parent change control — document-revision and training actions side by side, with separate status, separate deadlines.
Per-action deadlines
When change actions are extended, each gets a new deadline. The tool tracks all of them — and surfaces the earliest one at the top, so the team knows what's actually approaching.
Extension history visible
Items that have been extended once, twice, or more are flagged. Patterns of repeated extension become visible — useful for the team's own retrospectives.
Risk flags
Items near their deadline get an orange flag at the change-control level — even if only one of the change actions inside is at risk. No more discovering it the morning of.
Export for review
Snapshot exports for weekly status meetings and management reports — the same view the team works from, formatted for the audience that needs it.
In daily use

Deployed to the team I work with.

The tool sits alongside the validated system — not replacing it, not modifying it. The validated system is still the source of record. This is the operational layer where the daily question gets answered without anyone having to click through twelve change controls to find out which one has a deadline this week.

Built with the team's continuity in mind: documented well enough to maintain without me, and structured so future changes don't require my involvement.

Deployed

Used by the team for daily check-ins and weekly status reviews. Sits on top of the validated system as a read-only operational view. Documented for handoff.
