Open-access educational artifacts for regulated work — designed to make GMP environments and AI tooling navigable for newcomers and practitioners.

2 artifacts · open access · EN + KO

Within the Regulatory HCI research program, the same design principles that improve practitioner working conditions also produce educational artifacts as a natural extension. Tools that make accountability legible, scaffolds that preserve judgment, and interfaces that explain rather than automate — these are pedagogical gestures as much as design moves. The two artifacts below take that further: they exist outside any single workplace, freely available, written for the people regulated environments most often leave to figure things out alone.
📘 AI Literacy Guide · PDF

Claude, 이렇게 씁니다.
How to Actually Use Claude — A GMP Quality Lab AI Literacy Series

A practitioner-facing guide to using AI productively and safely in pharmaceutical GMP environments.

📄 15 pages 🌐 EN + KO 📂 Open access 🏢 GMP audience

Most AI guidance for regulated industries either treats AI as a generic productivity tool or as a regulatory liability — rarely as something that needs practitioner-level fluency. This cookbook fills that gap. It addresses what Claude is and isn't (capabilities, hallucination, when to escalate), what to never share (credentials, proprietary system data, raw GMP records), and how to write prompts for SOPs, deviation reports, CAPA, translation, and document review. The final chapter is a quick-reference card of starter lines for situations practitioners actually encounter. Designed for first-time Claude users and for managers building team workflows.

🎮 Pixel-Art Game · Web

DAY ONE: GMP
첫째 날: GMP — A Pixel Onboarding Game

A retro pixel-art game for newcomers entering pharmaceutical GMP environments. Five workday scenarios teach the core principles of regulated practice.

🕹 5 scenarios ⏱ ~15 minutes 🌐 EN + KO 📂 Open access

Most GMP onboarding takes the form of slide decks and SOP read-throughs — passive exposure to procedures the trainee won't internalize until they encounter them in real situations, often under pressure. DAY ONE: GMP reverses that order. The newcomer character (Jun) walks through one workday, encountering five decision points where compliance reasoning has to happen before formal documentation begins: gowning, material control, SOP retrieval, deviation reporting, and end-of-day sign-off. Each choice teaches something — there's no game over, but the explanation after each decision connects the situation to the underlying principle (ALCOA, deviation reporting obligations, the structural meaning of "validated procedure").

⚖ Why these matter

Education as a research output

Within Regulatory HCI, the gap between formal compliance training and the cognitive work practitioners actually do is a research finding — observable in SOP retrieval friction, in early-stage deviation reasoning, in the dissociation between confidence and convergence in root cause analysis. These artifacts translate that finding into practitioner-facing infrastructure: not a curriculum, but tools that meet newcomers and practitioners where they actually are. They are part of the same program as the papers and the deployed systems.
