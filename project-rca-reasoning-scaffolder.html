Expanding Causal Reasoning — AI as Comparison Enforcer

Initial RCA reasoning often narrows quickly under closure pressure. This scaffold was designed to keep multiple hypotheses visible long enough to delay premature convergence — testing whether interface scaffolding can keep causal reasoning open without automating the conclusion, flattening uncertainty, or displacing human judgment.

📋 Pilot study 👥 N=20 GMP practitioners 🤖 AI as comparison enforcer 🛠 Streamlit + Python
📖 Background
What is "AI as comparison enforcer" — and why RCA?
RCA (Root Cause Analysis) is the formal investigation process in GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) environments — FDA-enforced pharmaceutical production standards — used to explain why a deviation occurred. PAC (Premature Accountability Convergence) describes a recurring pattern in this process: investigation reasoning narrows toward an individual actor before procedural, contextual, and organizational contributors have been examined.

Most AI integrations in regulated work treat AI as an explanation generator — producing candidate causes for the analyst to evaluate. This scaffold reverses the role: AI here functions as a comparison enforcer. Rather than generating attributions, it detects when reasoning is converging too early and surfaces alternative pathways the analyst has not yet considered. The conclusion remains human; AI's job is to keep the comparison space open until the human is ready to close it.

01The problem — investigation pressure shapes reasoning
▼

Root Cause Analysis in GMP investigations is intended to explain why a deviation occurred, but closure pressure often narrows reasoning before alternatives are explored. This project examines a pattern consistent with Premature Accountability Convergence (PAC): investigation reasoning narrowing toward an individual actor before procedural, contextual, and organizational contributors have been examined.

The point is not only cognitive bias, but how interface and workflow design shape the reasoning process itself.

Investigation pressure points:

  • Closure pressure favors fast accountability over broad exploration
  • Standard forms underrepresent alternative causal pathways
  • Reasoning is documented late, after convergence has already occurred
  • Interface structure reinforces single-path explanation habits

If investigation environments structure reasoning, later accountability judgments may partly reflect interaction conditions. The question is not only who is blamed, but how the system made one explanatory path easier to follow than others.

Limitations. Same participant pool as Study 1 ensures consistent GMP baseline but introduces potential carry-over effects. Same-team recruitment may have inflated usability ratings.
✓ What worked
  • Five-stage flow slowed closure without blocking conclusions
  • PAC detection surfaced individual-blame language explicitly
  • AI reopening questions felt non-prescriptive
  • Reattribution to systemic causes did not collapse confidence
⚠ Remaining friction
  • Carry-over from Study 1 cannot be fully ruled out
  • Multi-instrument case may have favored systemic reattribution
  • Long-term adoption untested — adoption intent ≠ adoption
  • Real RCA workflows often allow less than 10 minutes for reasoning
04What this revealed
▼

The Layer 4 design move is to position AI as a comparison enforcer — detecting premature closure and reopening alternative pathways — rather than an explanation generator. This is a counter-design to typical AI integration in regulated work, which treats AI as a recommendation source.

"I had already decided on the answer — but the system kept asking what else could have caused it. So I had to actually look."

The reattribution finding (12 of 15 shifting from person-centered to systemic explanations) is consistent with the Study 1 finding that early convergence reflects environmental pressure rather than epistemic certainty. Together, the two studies argue that premature accountability convergence is a system-design problem, not a cognitive failing — and that interface scaffolding can productively delay closure without flattening human judgment.

All 20 participants reflected that their real-world investigation practice rarely allows ten minutes of focused causal reasoning — and more importantly, that the explanation they select in practice is not simply the most plausible one but the most documentable one. The scaffold's contribution is creating the time and structure for plausibility to enter the documentation.

Companion: Study 1 — establishes the empirical baseline (without intervention) showing that reasoning naturally broadens with time, motivating the scaffold as a structured way to provide that time inside investigation workflows.
