Reagent Inventory & Expiry Tool

Stock and expiry tracking for hundreds of reagents — with automatic email alerts based on lead-time rules and expiry windows. No electronic system existed for this; the team had been doing it from logbooks and Excel.

The problem

Hundreds of reagents. No electronic system. Manual checks that arrive too late.

Inventory tracking for reagents at the site was being done from logbooks and Excel files. That worked when the volume was small. But with hundreds of distinct reagents — across different vendors, lead times, storage conditions, and expiry windows — manual checking became unreliable. By the time someone noticed stock was low, it was often already too late to reorder without disrupting work, especially for reagents that ship from overseas with multi-week lead times.

The expiry side had the same problem. Without a structured reminder system, expiring reagents would either be quietly used past their date, or quietly discarded after expiry — neither outcome is good. Both came from the same root: no one had time to manually check hundreds of items every day, and the system wasn't surfacing what needed attention.

Before

Reagent owner manually opens logbook / Excel, scrolls through hundreds of rows, eyeballs stock counts, cross-references vendor lead times, checks expiry dates one by one. Most days, no time to do this thoroughly. Action happens when something runs out — already too late.

After

Owner does nothing daily. The system checks every reagent against the rules every morning. When a threshold is crossed — stock low for the reagent's lead-time class, or expiry approaching — an email arrives in the owner's inbox, naming the reagent and the reason. Action happens before depletion or expiry.

What it does

Watches every reagent every day. Emails the owner only when action is needed.

Single source: Google Sheets
All reagents live in one Sheet the team already knew how to update. No new system to learn, no migration, no risk of two-source drift.
Stock-based alerts
Overseas reagents notify when stock ≤ 3. Domestic reagents notify when stock ≤ 1. Thresholds reflect actual lead times, not arbitrary defaults.
Expiry cadence
3 months, 1 month, 1 week, 1 day before expiry — four reminders escalating in urgency. The owner can act early without needing to remember.
Per-reagent owner routing
Each reagent has a designated owner. Notifications go directly to them — no team-wide blasts, no missed alerts.
Visible inventory status
Anyone can open the Sheet to see live stock and expiry status with color coding — green sufficient, orange reorder, red expiring.
Configurable rules
Thresholds and reminder cadences are editable in the Sheet itself. No code change needed when policy changes — the team owns the parameters.
A note on the choice of tools

Google Sheets, not Python.

This tool isn't built in Python, or Streamlit, or any framework I use elsewhere. It runs on Google Sheets with Apps Script. That choice was deliberate.

Design principle

The reagent owners were already using Sheets daily. A Python app would have meant a new tool to learn, a new login to remember, and a system that depended on me to maintain. Sheets + Apps Script gave them automation inside the system they already trusted — same interface, same login, same place they were already going every morning. The notification logic is the only thing that's new; everything else they were already doing.

The principle isn't "simpler is better" — it's "use what fits the problem, not what's impressive." For this problem, the team's existing workflow was the right substrate. Adding an automation layer on top was the smallest change that solved the actual issue.

In daily use

Deployed to the team I work with.

Used by the reagent owners across the team. The Sheet is updated when reagents are received or used — the same way it always was — and the Apps Script does the rest. Documentation lives alongside the Sheet so future team members can modify rules without my involvement.

Deployed

Used daily by reagent owners. Notification rules editable by the team. Includes reproduction guide and team training documentation — the tool is not dependent on my presence to maintain.
