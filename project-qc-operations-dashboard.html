QC Operations Dashboard

A live view of the team's current QC operations — tests in progress, pass rates, sample throughput, and approaching deadlines — replacing the scattered Excel files that used to hold this picture.

The problem

Twelve Excel files, one team, no shared view.

Before this tool, the team's daily picture of QC operations lived in roughly a dozen Excel files — one for stability tests, one for release testing, one for OOS investigations, one for sample throughput. To answer a simple question like "what's running right now and what's due this week?", you'd open four or five of them, cross-reference manually, and hope nothing had been updated by someone else in the meantime.

Most of the time it worked. Sometimes it didn't — a missed pull date, an overlooked deadline, a status that had drifted from reality. The cost of fragmentation wasn't dramatic. It just quietly fell on whoever happened to be holding the picture in their head.

What it does

A single live view of operations across the team.

Live test status
Every active test across the team — current stage, day count, lot, assigned analyst — updated as the day moves.
Pull-date timeline
Upcoming pull dates and deadlines surfaced in calendar form. Items approaching their window are flagged before they slip.
Throughput trends
Tests completed by week, by assay category, by analyst. Catches workload imbalances and seasonal patterns the team couldn't see before.
Exception surfacing
OOS results, missed deadlines, and tests stalled at one stage are surfaced as visible flags — not buried in a row of a spreadsheet.
Drill-down to records
Every metric and chart links back to the underlying test records. Aggregate numbers and individual cases stay connected.
Export for review
Weekly and monthly snapshots exportable for QA review and management reporting — the same view, in the format reviewers expect.
In daily use

Deployed to the team I work with.

This is the dashboard the team checks first thing in the morning and last thing before leaving. It replaced the scattered Excel files entirely — not by replicating them in a fancier format, but by giving everyone the same picture of operations at the same time.

It's built with sustainability in mind: documented enough that the team can keep using it after I leave, and the underlying data model is plain enough that future modifications don't depend on me being there to make them.

Deployed

Used daily by the QC team. Reproducible from documentation; not a black box dependent on my presence to maintain. Expanded over time from a stability-test view into the full operations dashboard it is now.
