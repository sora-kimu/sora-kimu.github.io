Most AI-literacy material is written for office workers. This one isn't. It's written for QC teams in a regulated manufacturing environment — where the rule is "no credentials, no Veeva, no shared drives," where every action has an audit trail, and where ALCOA+ data integrity isn't optional. Four chapters covering what AI can and can't do, how to keep it safe, how to use it for the documents that matter (SOPs, deviations, CAPA, translation), and how managers can build small workflows without writing code.

DAY ONE: GMP

A pixel onboarding game for pharmaceutical newcomers · ~15 min.

EN KO

Newcomers in pharmaceutical GMP environments typically learn by watching seniors and hoping nothing breaks. This is gentler: a character named Jun walks through their first day across five scenarios — gowning, material verification, SOP retrieval under pressure, deviation reporting, and end-of-day sign-off — each with four choices and an explanation after every pick. The game doesn't punish wrong answers; it shows why one path is more aligned with regulated practice than the others.

1 Gowning areaFollowing the documented sequence
2 Material verificationWhat gets checked, and why
3 SOP retrieval under pressureAsking vs. acting on memory
4 Deviation reportingWhat counts, what doesn't
5 End-of-day sign-offClosing out the workday properly

— Sustainability documentation

The tools have to outlast me

A deployed tool that depends on its builder to maintain is a liability, not infrastructure. Every operational tool on the Tools page ships with documentation that lets the team keep it running after I leave.

Internal · per deployed tool
🔄

Reproduction guides & training materials

Every operational tool ships with handoff documentation. Nothing depends on me to keep working.

One of the easiest ways for internal tools to fail is for their builder to leave. The team loses the tool, or — worse — keeps using it without being able to fix it when something breaks. My answer is structural: every operational tool I've deployed comes with four documents that together let someone else maintain, modify, and teach the tool without me being there. The documentation is part of the tool's design, not an afterthought.

1 READMEWhat it does, why it exists
2 SETUPHow to reproduce it from scratch
3 TRAININGHow to onboard new users
4 HANDOFFWhat to do when I'm not here

The documentation lives internally with the tool, not publicly — it references the regulated environment it operates in. Contact me for review if you'd like to see the structure or methodology.
