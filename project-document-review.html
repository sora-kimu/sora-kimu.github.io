Document Auto-Review Assistant

An AI that surfaces formatting drift, missing references, and structural inconsistencies in draft documents — at the moment of writing, before they accumulate. The AI highlights only. It never auto-corrects.

The problem

Every analyst writes a little differently. Hundreds of documents later, that's a problem.

Pharmaceutical documents — method validation reports, analytical test reports, SOPs, change records — share a common formal structure. Master templates exist. But the people who actually write the drafts are different people, with different writing habits, different memories of what the template requires, and different interpretations of what "consistent" means.

Most of the time the differences are small. A heading style here, a unit-spacing convention there, a glossary term used one way in one document and another way in another. Each individual deviation is trivial. The problem is that they accumulate. By the time the document set is large enough to matter, you're staring at hundreds of files that don't quite match each other — and the only way to fix it is a mass revision project nobody has time for.

The cleaner answer is upstream: catch the deviations at the moment of drafting, not months later in a revision sprint.

What it does

Reads draft documents against the master template. Surfaces drift, never corrects it.

Master-document trained
The AI is fine-tuned on validated master documents — formatting conventions, terminology, structural patterns. The draft is compared against that learned baseline.
Inline highlights
Deviations are highlighted directly in the document — yellow for format, orange for unit conventions, red for missing references. Authors see exactly what differs.
Reason given, not just flag
Each highlighted item shows what the master template uses and why this differs. "Heading style mismatch — master uses bold + numbered" rather than just "issue."
Categorized by type
FORMAT, UNITS, TERMINOLOGY, REFERENCE — each issue tagged so the author knows which deserve urgent attention and which are stylistic.
Author keeps the decision
Nothing is auto-corrected. The AI flags; the author chooses to accept, ignore, or correct manually. The decision authority stays with the human writer.
Drift trends
Over time the tool shows which deviations are common — useful for updating training, refining the master template, or refreshing internal guidelines.
Why the AI doesn't auto-correct

Augmentation over automation — especially here.

Building this tool, the most tempting design choice was the wrong one: let the AI auto-correct everything it finds. The code was actually easier that way. I deliberately didn't do it.

Design principle

In a regulated domain, the document is the legal record. An auto-corrected sentence is a sentence the human author didn't fully read, didn't choose to change, and didn't make a judgment about. That violates how regulatory documents are supposed to be authored — every assertion has to be a human assertion, made knowingly. So the AI's job here is to surface, not decide. The author still owns every word in the document. The AI just makes drift visible at the moment of writing, when the cost of fixing it is one keystroke instead of one revision sprint.

In daily use

Deployed to the team I work with.

Used by analysts during document drafting — runs as a review pass before the document goes to the next reviewer. Picks up most of the small drift that used to surface only in mass-revision cycles. The team owns when to use it and which highlights to act on.

Deployed

Used during document drafting by analysts. Includes reproduction guide and team training documentation — and explicit guidance on the augmentation principle, so future users understand why the AI flags but doesn't decide.
