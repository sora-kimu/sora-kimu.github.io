Bilingual Document Translation

Korean–English translation pipeline for GMP documents, with a team-managed glossary that keeps terminology consistent across the document family — so the same Korean source clause doesn't end up with five different English translations across hundreds of files.

The problem

Same Korean source. Five English translations later, you have a mass-revision project.

GMP documents at this site are written in Korean and translated to English for regulatory submission and partner exchange. Different analysts translate at different times. They're all competent — but they're not the same person, and a Korean term like 검액 ends up as "sample solution" in one document, "test solution" in another, and "specimen solution" in a third. 평균 becomes "average" here, "mean" there. 판정 becomes "judgement" sometimes, "evaluation" other times.

Each individual choice is plausible. The problem is the aggregate. Once a partner reviews the document family and notes the inconsistency, the team is looking at a mass revision project across hundreds of documents — work that could have been avoided if the terminology had been consistent the first time. The real cost isn't the original translation. It's the downstream revision.

The fix is small but structural: maintain a glossary, make every translator use it, and surface what the glossary says inline during translation.

What it does

Korean in. English out. Glossary in the middle.

Team-managed glossary
412 terms maintained by the team — Korean source on one side, agreed English translation on the other. Changes are reviewed and tracked.
Consistent term substitution
During translation, every glossary term is replaced with its agreed English equivalent — no analyst-by-analyst drift, no five-ways-to-say-the-same-thing.
Format conventions preserved
Numeric ranges spaced as "84 ~ 123 %", acceptance criteria as "shall be not less than / not more than", evaluation as "Evaluation: Pass" — the conventions the team agreed on, applied automatically.
Conflict flagging
When a translator uses a term that doesn't match the glossary, it's highlighted before the document goes to review. No mystery deviations slipping through.
Glossary growth tracking
As new terms appear in the source documents, they're surfaced for the team to add to the glossary. The vocabulary evolves with the work.
Bilingual document output
Final output is a side-by-side Korean–English document in the team's standard Word template — same formatting, same fonts, same paragraph rules every time.
Where the value sits

The glossary is the product. The translation is the byproduct.

Most translation tools position themselves as language tools — fast, fluent, machine-translated. This one is a different shape: a terminology-consistency tool that happens to translate. The fluency of any single output matters less than the consistency across the document family.

Design principle

In regulated work, the same concept must be the same word across hundreds of files. A translator who's a beautiful writer but uses a different English term in document #7 than they used in document #1 has created a worse outcome than a translator who's mechanical but consistent. So the tool's job isn't to write elegantly — it's to enforce a shared vocabulary the team has agreed to. The glossary is where the team's actual translation decisions live; the tool is just the delivery mechanism.
